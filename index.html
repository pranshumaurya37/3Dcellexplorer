Cell component / 0101

Nucleus

Information center

The nucleus stores most of the cell’s DNA and helps coordinate gene expression. Its instructions are copied into RNA that can guide protein production.

What it does

DNA stays protected inside the nuclear envelope. When a gene is needed, the cell makes an RNA copy that can leave through a nuclear pore.

Structure

A double membrane encloses chromatin and the nucleolus, with pores in the envelope.

Works with

RNA leaves through pores and can be translated by ribosomes.

Think of it as: the cell’s instruction library. The nucleus coordinates activity; it does not control every event like a tiny brain.

A note on the model

One cell, many variations.

This is a simplified animal cell. Eukaryotic cells share core features, but their contents vary by organism and job. Plant cells, for example, have chloroplasts and a large central vacuole; plants often use vacuoles for digestion rather than having typical animal-cell lysosomes.
